Real raise calculator: did your raise actually beat inflation?

Save100ThisMonth toolsUpdated October 10, 2026Runs in your browser

A raise feels like progress. But if prices rose faster than your paycheck, you can earn more dollars and still afford less. Economists call the difference your real wage.

This calculator compares your raise to inflation and tells you, in plain numbers, whether your buying power went up or down.

Use the latest 12-month CPI change from the Bureau of Labor Statistics for the inflation figure.

Nominal vs real: the 30-second explanation

Nominal is the number on your paycheck. Real is what that paycheck can buy.

If your pay rises 3% but prices rise 3.5%, your nominal pay went up and your real pay went down. You are, in effect, about half a percent poorer.

The formula

Subtracting inflation from your raise gives a quick estimate. The precise version divides instead:

real change = (1 + raise) / (1 + inflation) - 1 example (1.03 / 1.035) - 1 = -0.48% break-even you need a raise equal to inflation just to stand still

What to do if your raise lost to inflation

  1. Bring data to the conversation. Market salary ranges for your role are more persuasive than feelings.
  2. Ask about the whole package. Retirement match, bonus, training budget and remote days all have dollar value.
  3. Protect your savings. Make sure cash earns a competitive rate. See our high-yield savings checklist.
  4. Cut leaks first. Run your recurring costs through the subscription creep calculator.
A raise that trails inflation is a pay cut with better marketing.

Personal inflation is not the headline number

The official rate is an average basket. Your own inflation depends on what you buy.

If rent, insurance or childcare make up most of your budget and those rose faster, your personal inflation may be higher than the headline. Try entering a higher figure to stress-test your result.

How we tested this tool

What we checked
  • Inflation is clamped to a realistic -20% to +50% range. This prevents a division by zero when someone enters -100%.
  • Raise is clamped to -50% to +200%, so a mistyped extra digit cannot produce an absurd result.
  • Negative salary values are treated as zero.
  • Hand-traced example: a 3% raise against 3.5% inflation gives -0.48% real change, matching the formula above.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the inflation rate?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Consumer Price Index monthly. Use the 12-month change that matches your raise period.

Does this include taxes?

No. It compares gross pay to prices. A higher salary can also move part of your income into a higher bracket, which reduces the take-home gain.

Is my salary data stored?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser.
