Subscription Creep Calculator: what your subscriptions really cost

Save100ThisMonth toolsUpdated October 2026Runs in your browser

Nobody signs up for a $1,500-a-year habit. You sign up for a $9.99 trial. Then another. Then a price bump you never notice. That slow pile-up has a name: subscription creep.

This tool adds it all up. List every recurring charge, and you will see the monthly total, the 1, 5 and 10-year cost, which services cost you the most, and what the same money could have grown into.

Why this number surprises almost everyone

Monthly pricing is designed to feel light. Your brain compares $14.99 to a coffee, not to a car payment.

But subscriptions do not behave like coffee. They renew automatically, they rarely get cheaper, and they stack. Five average services can easily pass $80 a month. That is close to $1,000 a year, every year, for things you may barely use.

How the calculator works

1. Monthly and yearly total

We add every price you enter. The yearly figure is simply the monthly total times 12.

2. Five and ten-year cost

If you add an expected price increase, each year costs a little more than the last. With zero, it is straight multiplication.

3. Opportunity cost

This is the interesting one. It asks: if that same monthly amount went into savings or investments instead, what could it grow to? We use standard monthly compounding.

monthly rate r = annual return / 12 future value FV = monthly total x ((1 + r)^months - 1) / r example $80/month, 5%, 120 months -> about $12,400
The return is an assumption, not a promise. Investments can lose value. We default to 5% only to illustrate the idea. Change it, or set it to your savings account rate for a no-risk comparison.

Five ways to cut the total this month

  1. Pull 3 months of statements. Search for recurring charges. Most people find at least one they forgot.
  2. Apply the 30-day test. Did you use it in the last 30 days? If not, cancel. You can always come back.
  3. Rotate, do not stack. Keep one streaming service at a time and switch every few months.
  4. Check for duplicates. Cloud storage, music and news often overlap with bundles you already pay for.
  5. Calendar every free trial. Set a reminder two days before it converts to paid.
The cheapest subscription is the one you cancel before it renews.

Annual or monthly billing?

Annual plans often save 15 to 20%. But they only win if you keep the service all year.

Quick rule: divide the annual price by the monthly price. That is your break-even month. If you are not confident you will still use it after that month, stay monthly.

Frequently asked questions

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Close the tab and it is gone.

Why include an investment return?

It shows the trade-off. Money spent on unused services is money that could have grown. Use your savings rate if you prefer a risk-free comparison.

Should I cancel everything?

No. Keep what you genuinely use and value. The goal is intentional spending, not zero spending.

What counts as a subscription?

Anything that renews automatically: streaming, music, apps, cloud storage, gyms, meal kits, software, memberships and delivery passes.
