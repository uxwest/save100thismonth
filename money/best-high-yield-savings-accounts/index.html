Best high-yield savings accounts

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialRates checked Oct 10, 202613 min

Every best-savings-account list goes stale in a month, because rates move weekly. Here is the current picture, the live sources to verify it, and the six checks to run before you move a dollar.

The baseline: the FDIC average

The FDIC publishes the national average savings rate weekly. When we last checked it sat around 0.41% APY, while leading online accounts advertised 4.0% to 5.0%.

Check the live number. Rates move weekly. Open FDIC national rates and use today's figure.

What the APY banner hides

APY is the right number to compare, but it is variable. Three tactics distort it:

Promotional windows

A headline rate that lasts 60-90 days, then drops. The drop lives in the footnotes.

Balance caps

The rate may only cover the first $1,000 or $10,000. A "3% rate" on $1,000 is closer to a $30 bonus.

Behaviour-linked rates

Some require direct deposit or a linked checking account. Miss one month and the rate slips silently.

The promo-rate trap

Bank A advertises 5.00% for three months, then 3.50%. Bank B pays a flat 4.30%. Bank B wins over a year.

blended = (5.00 x 3 + 3.50 x 9) / 12 = 3.88% flat = 4.30% The advertised winner loses.

Fees: the silent APY killer

On a $2,000 balance, a 4.25% rate earns about $85 a year. A $5 monthly fee takes $60 of it. Subtract fees from interest — that is the only comparison that matters.

  • Monthly maintenance and what waives it
  • Inactivity or low-balance fees
  • Outgoing wire and transfer fees

FDIC insurance, decoded

FDIC insurance protects deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category.

  1. Confirm it is an insured bank on FDIC BankFind.
  2. Your account is held there, not just marketed by an app.
Red flag. "Funds held at our partner bank" with no name. If you can't verify the bank, you can't verify the cover.

The 30-minute method

  1. Get the live numbers from the FDIC page and one rankings site.
  2. Copy the facts — APY, standard rate, fees, transfer rules — from each bank's own page.
  3. Score on six checks, fees and insurance double-weighted.
  4. Act. Open the winner, test a small transfer, automate a deposit.

FAQ

What is a good rate right now?

Compare against the FDIC national average, which moves weekly. A steady rate well above it, with no fee, is competitive.

Are online banks safe?

If FDIC-insured, deposits are protected to the limits. Verify on BankFind.

How much should I keep in a HYSA?

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. Our runway calculator gives a personal number.
