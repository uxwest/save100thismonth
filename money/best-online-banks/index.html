What an online bank actually is

An online bank is a real bank. Same charter, same deposit insurance, same rules. Just without branches.

That is the whole model. No buildings, no tellers, lower costs. Some of that comes back to you as interest or, more often, as the absence of fees.

The category that confuses everyone

Some money apps look like banks, talk like banks and even mention FDIC. But they are not banks. They are fintech companies that park your cash with a partner bank.

That can be perfectly safe. The problem is when the app will not name the bank. You cannot verify insurance on a noun you cannot see.

Two-minute testSearch the institution on FDIC BankFind. If it returns an insured bank, you are fine. If it returns nothing, keep reading until you find who really holds your cash.

The 7-point checklist

Score each candidate from 1 to 5. Weight fees and insurance double. Pick the highest total, not the highest single rate.

Fees that eat your interest

Fees behave like negative interest. On a small balance they usually win.

Consider $2,000. Moving from near-zero to a competitive rate might earn you around $85 extra a year. A $5 monthly fee takes $60 of that.

net gain = (balance x new rate) - (balance x old rate) - yearly fees example = $85 - $0.20 - $60 = roughly $24.80

Fees to check before opening anything

  • Monthly maintenance and the conditions to waive it
  • Inactivity or low-balance charges
  • Excess withdrawal or transfer fees
  • Outgoing wires and expedited transfers

Why the APY moves

A savings APY is a price, not a promise. Banks raise it to attract cash and cut it when they have enough.

That has two consequences. First, today's number is a snapshot. Second, promotional rates deserve extra reading: many apply for a window, to new money, up to a cap.

Ask not which bank pays the most today. Ask which bank pays a sensible standard rate on your balance, six months from now.

Two banks, two stories. One advertises a headline rate that drops after 90 days. The other offers a boring flat rate with no games.

Over a full year, the boring bank often wins. Here is the arithmetic:

blended = (promo x months + standard x remaining) / 12 example = (5.00 x 3 + 3.50 x 9) / 12 = 3.88% vs 4.30% flat

FDIC, decoded

FDIC insurance covers deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. If the bank fails, insured depositors are made whole.

Two checks confirm you are covered:

  1. The institution is an insured bank. Verify on FDIC BankFind.
  2. Your account is held there. Not just marketed by an app.
Ceiling alertIf one ownership category at one bank exceeds $250,000, the extra is not insured. Split institutions or use different ownership categories.

The 30-minute method

  1. Minutes 0 to 10. Shortlist five. Two online banks, one large bank's online arm, one credit union and one fintech app.
  2. Minutes 10 to 20. Copy the facts. From each bank's own site: current APY, standard APY, fee schedule, transfer rules.
  3. Minutes 20 to 28. Score them. Use the seven checks. Double the weight on fees and insurance.
  4. Minutes 28 to 30. Act. Open the winner, test with a small transfer, then automate a monthly deposit.

Four mistakes that cost money

  • Chasing a top rate at a provider that will not name its bank
  • Ignoring a fee that cancels the rate advantage
  • Moving everything before testing a small transfer
  • Never checking the rate again after opening the account

Frequently asked questions

Are online banks safe?

Yes, if they are FDIC-insured banks. The insurance, not the building, protects you. Verify the institution on FDIC BankFind.

Do I need both an online bank and a traditional one?

Many households keep both. Online for savings, a local branch or credit union for cash deposits and in-person needs.

How fast can I move money?

Standard ACH transfers usually take one to three business days. Wires are same-day but often carry a fee.

Is the highest rate always the best choice?

No. A promo that expires can pay less over a year than a steady rate, and fees can erase a rate advantage entirely.

What if a fintech app holds my money?

Some apps are not banks. They place deposits at partner banks. Safe only if the app names the bank so you can verify coverage.
