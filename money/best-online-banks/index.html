Best Online Banks: What Actually Matters

Money & Better Returns · Guide · For US readers

Quick answer: The best online bank for you is the one with no monthly fees, FDIC insurance, free/cheap ATM access where you actually live, and an APY that’s competitive on both checking and savings. Everything else — slick apps, referral bonuses — is secondary.
On this pageChecklist · Trade-offs of online-only · Mistakes · FAQ

The checklist

Monthly maintenance fee$0Why pay to hold your own money?
Overdraft policyNo overdraft fees, or a decline-instead policyOverdraft fees are one of the most expensive bank fees
ATM networkFree withdrawals at ATMs you actually useOut-of-network fees can be $3–5 each
APY on savingsVerify current rate on the bank’s own siteRates change; published lists go stale
FDIC insuranceDirect, or clearly disclosed partner-bank structureVerify with the FDIC

SupportReal humans, reasonable hoursYou’ll need them eventually

Trade-offs of online-only banks

  • Pro: Lower overhead usually means better rates and fewer fees.
  • Con: No physical branches — cash deposits may be limited or impossible.
  • Con: Transfers to outside banks can take 1–3 business days; keep a small buffer at a traditional bank if timing matters.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing for a signup bonus without checking ongoing fees.
  • Assuming every “online bank” app holds your deposit in an insured bank — some are fintech middlemen. Read the disclosures.
  • Ignoring what happens when you need a cashier’s check or wire quickly.

FAQ

Do I need both a traditional and an online bank?

Many people do: an online bank for the bulk of savings, and a local bank or credit union for cash deposits and in-person needs.

How do I verify a bank is legitimate?

Search the institution on the FDIC’s BankFind tool and confirm the account is opened directly with the insured entity.
