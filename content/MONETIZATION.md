# Monetization playbook: AdSense + Google rankings

Honest framing: nobody can guarantee AdSense approval or rankings. These are the factors that most often decide both.

## 1. Before you apply to AdSense

- [ ] Serve the site on the real domain save100thismonth.com (not the github.io URL). AdSense approves sites, and the domain must be yours.
- [ ] Replace placeholder emails (privacy@ / contact) with working mailboxes.
- [ ] Keep About and Contact reachable. The footer is now minimal by design, but these pages still exist and are in the sitemap. Consider linking them in the footer again if review feedback mentions navigation.
- [ ] At least 15 to 20 substantial, original pages. Today: 2 fully rewritten flagships, 9 legacy articles still to migrate, 4 new tools.
- [ ] No empty ad boxes. Placeholders are now hidden with CSS.
- [ ] Install a Google-certified consent tool (CMP) for EEA/UK/US-state visitors before ads run.
- [ ] Create ads.txt ONLY with the exact line AdSense gives you after signup.
- [ ] Add a 1200x630 PNG/JPG og:image per page. Facebook does not render SVG previews.

## 2. Google ranking basics

- Submit sitemap.xml in Google Search Console after the domain is live.
- Keep updating the dateModified only when content truly changes.
- Each article targets one main keyword and several sub-questions answered in H2/H3 and FAQ.
- Internal links: every article links to its category, two related guides and one tool.
- Finance is a YMYL topic. Trust signals matter: named sources, clear corrections policy and, ideally, real author bylines with credentials.

## 3. Revenue model

- Facebook traffic (your posts, link in first comment) usually earns lower ad RPM than US organic search traffic. Use Facebook for reach; build search traffic for margin.
- Highest-value topics on this site: car insurance, savings and banking, credit cards. Prioritise these for new content.
- Tools pages earn engagement and shares. Use them as Facebook hooks, then link to the related high-value guide inside the tool page.

## 4. Ad placement once approved (do not over-place)

- 1 unit after the 2nd or 3rd H2 in articles
- 1 unit mid-article on long guides
- 1 unit in the sidebar under related posts (desktop)
- Prefer fewer, well-placed units; too many hurts Core Web Vitals, user trust and approval.
