Best Free Website Builder for a Business: What “Free” Really Costs

Free website builders genuinely exist, and for some businesses they are the right answer. But “free” always includes at least one of three things: platform branding on your site, a subdomain instead of your own domain, or a limit that appears exactly where you need it most — payment processing. This guide shows what each free route actually gives you, where the hidden costs sit, and which option scales without embarrassing you in front of a customer.

The static-site route deserves more attention than it usually gets, because it is the only one that gives you a real custom domain, fast pages and no platform branding at no monthly cost. The trade is that you handle the setup yourself, or pay someone once to do it. For a simple business site — services, location, contact, hours — that trade is often excellent value.

Hidden costs to add up before choosing

  • Domain. Around ten to twenty dollars a year. Not optional if you want to look like a real business.
  • Removing platform branding. Usually the specific feature behind the first paid tier. Check whether branding actually appears on your chosen plan.
  • Payment processing. Some free plans either charge a commission or block checkout entirely. If you sell on the site, this matters more than any other line.
  • Storage and bandwidth caps that you only discover when your images fail to load for customers.
  • Your time. DIY is free in dollars and expensive in hours, and that is a real cost worth counting.
real_annual_cost = domain + (plan_renewal) + payment_fees + your_hours × your_hourly_value note: a free plan with a 3% sales commission is expensive the moment you sell anything

A recommendation by situation

Validating an idea. Use a free tier or a one-page static site. The point is to find out whether anyone cares, not to build an empire.

A local service business. A simple static one-pager plus a complete business listing on search and social platforms will outperform both, and cost almost nothing. Customers searching for a plumber or a salon mostly want a phone number, opening hours, location and proof you are real.

Selling online from day one. Budget for a paid e-commerce tier. Free plans rarely process payments well, and the commission can exceed the subscription cost within weeks.

“A custom domain costs about the price of two coffees a year and changes how customers read everything else on the page.”

The single best small investment

If you take one thing from this guide, take this: buy a custom domain. It is the cheapest possible upgrade in perceived legitimacy. A business address that ends in a builder's name reads as a hobby, regardless of how good the site is. Pointing a real domain at a free static host or a free tier takes minutes and removes the most visible signal that you are improvising.

Frequently asked questions

Is a subdomain bad for business?

It looks less professional, it is harder to remember, and you do not control it. For any customer-facing business, a custom domain costing ten to twenty dollars a year is the best small investment available.

Can I start free and upgrade later?

Usually yes with hosted builders, but check the export options first. Some lock your content into a format that is painful to migrate away from.

Do free plans include e-commerce?

Sometimes partially, often with a commission or without full checkout. If selling is core to your business rather than a future idea, budget for a paid tier from the start.

Is a static site too difficult for a non-technical owner?

It can be, though templates and one-off help make it manageable. The advantage is real: a custom domain, fast pages, no branding and no monthly fee. If you would rather not touch it, a low-cost builder tier may be simpler overall.

What do I actually need on a small business site?

What you do, where you are, how to contact you, your hours, and evidence you are legitimate — reviews, photos, licence details. Extra pages are nice; those five things are the business.
