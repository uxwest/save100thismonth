Wix vs Squarespace for a Portfolio: Which One Fits How You Work?

For a portfolio site, the platform decision is less about features than about temperament. One gives you a freeform canvas that will do anything you can imagine, including look messy. The other gives you structured templates that are difficult to make ugly and slightly harder to make unusual. This guide compares them on the things that actually matter — design control, how each behaves under your own habits, and the real three-year cost once promotional pricing ends.

Read the last row carefully, because it applies to both. Your text and images can be exported. The design you built cannot. If you later switch, you are rebuilding the site, and that should factor into the decision more than any single feature.

The real cost over three years

Do not compare promotional prices. Take each platform's renewal price for the plan you actually need, add a custom domain, and multiply by three. A plan that looks a few dollars a month cheaper in year one frequently converges or inverts by year three, because introductory discounts expire while the renewal price does not.

three_year_cost = (renewal_price × 3) + (domain_cost × 3) + any_app_or_addon_fees compare the RENEWAL figure, never the first-year discount

Use a subscription calculator with the monthly and annual figures you have verified to see the actual difference, and remember to check whether the plan you need to sell work — not the cheapest tier — is the one you are pricing.

Which one suits which portfolio

Wix tends to win for unusual layouts, multilingual sites, very specific visual ideas, and anyone who wants to reach for an app store to add functionality. It rewards people who enjoy tinkering and penalises those who do not, because nothing stops you from producing a page that looks like five different websites stacked together.

Squarespace tends to win for photography and design portfolios where typography and image presentation carry the brand, and for people who want the result to look considered without spending weekends on it. The structure that limits you is the same structure that keeps the output coherent.

“One platform asks what you want to build. The other asks whether you would rather it just looked right.”

Before you commit to either

  • Check the current plan prices and included features on each provider's own pricing page, because both change regularly.
  • Confirm you can connect your own domain on the tier you are considering — a portfolio on a subdomain looks like a draft.
  • Test the editor on a real page during the trial, not just a template preview, because that is where the daily friction lives.
  • Note the refund and cancellation terms before paying annually, which is usually the only way to get the advertised rate.
  • Decide how much of the design work you want to do yourself. This single question usually settles the choice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch platforms later?

Your content — text and images — can be exported from either. The design cannot, so budget for a rebuild if you switch. This is the strongest argument for choosing carefully the first time.

Is the free plan enough for a portfolio?

Free tiers typically place platform branding on your site and use a subdomain. That is acceptable for a draft or a learning project, but not for showing work to clients who are deciding whether to hire you.

Which is better for photographers?

Squarespace's gallery-first templates and typography tend to suit photography portfolios with less effort. Wix gives more control if you have a specific layout in mind and the patience to build it.

Do I need e-commerce?

Only if you sell prints or services directly. Squarespace's built-in commerce is cleaner for simple catalogues; Wix's app marketplace is broader if you need integrations.

How much should I budget annually?

Price the plan you need at renewal, not the discounted first year, and add a custom domain. Compare the monthly and annual options with a calculator before choosing the annual commitment.
