Best credit card for travel: pick by your trips, not the points

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialUpdated October 10, 202611 min read

A points balance feels like wealth until you try to spend it.

Blackout dates. Portal-only credits. Transfer partners you never fly. Devaluations announced by email.

The travellers who profit from travel cards share one habit: they run the fee-versus-value maths on their own trips before applying, not on the brochure's ideal customer.

The three card families

Know which game you are playing before you apply.

Pick your family
FamilyWins whenLoses when
Transferable pointsYou compare partners and book aheadYou redeem at 1 cent for gift cards
Airline co-brandOne airline, checked bags, companionsYou book whoever is cheapest
No-fee travelOne or two trips a year, simplicityYou chase lounges and elite status

Most travellers belong in the first or third family. The airline card pays only for real loyalists who fly one airline monthly and check a bag every time.

The fee-versus-value equation

Add up only the value you would buy anyway. Then subtract the annual fee.

net value = usable credits + lounge value + (points x your redemption) + foreign fee savings - annual fee rule: if it is negative two years in a row, downgrade

Premium cards justify their fee through three things: travel credits, lounge access and status perks. Use two of the three and the maths works. Use none and you have bought an expensive ornament.

The vague list problemIf you cannot name the specific credits you will use this year, the fee is pure cost. Do not apply on hope.

Point values, honestly

Issuers advertise values above two cents per point. Real redemptions cluster lower.

What a point is usually worth
RedemptionTypical valueVerdict
Gift cards and cash backabout 1 centWaste
Issuer travel portal1 to 1.5 centsFine
Transfer to airline partners, premium cabins booked early1.2 to 2 cents or moreBest use
Last-minute economy bookingsoften under 1 centValue collapses
Points are a currency the issuer can devalue overnight. Earn them to spend them, not to collect them.

Before choosing points over a flat cash-back card, estimate your last 12 months of redemptions. Most casual travellers earn more, with less effort, from cash back.

Foreign transaction fees: non-negotiable

A 3% foreign transaction fee on $5,000 of annual overseas spending costs $150. That is more than many annual fees.

Any card you carry across a border must charge zero percent. No exceptions.

Also check

  • Dynamic currency conversion. Always choose to pay in the local currency.
  • ATM policies abroad, which often differ from purchase rules
  • Whether “no foreign fee” covers purchases only or cash-like transactions too

Lounges: count visits, not prestige

Unlimited access sounds luxurious until you count.

Two domestic trips a year with short layovers might mean two visits. At a generous $30 of perceived value each, that is $60 against a $395 fee.

Frequent long-haul travellers with delays extract hundreds. Everyone else subsidises them.

Audit last year's actual itineraries before paying for a network you will wave at through glass.

Cash back versus points: the crossover

A flat 2% cash-back card with no annual fee and no foreign fee is the rational default for most households.

Points win when travel spending is high, redemptions are planned and partners match airlines you actually fly.

Simple test: if your last three redemptions averaged under 1.5 cents per point, switch your mental winner to cash back and stop paying a premium fee for complexity.

Compare properlyTake your real spending mix and subtract any annual fee. Simplicity compounds. Orphan points expire.

Downgrade triggers worth calendaring

Set a reminder 30 days before each renewal. Ask four questions.

  1. Did I use the credits? If not, they were decoration.
  2. Did I visit lounges more than four times?
  3. Did my redemptions clear 1.5 cents per point?
  4. Did my travel actually decrease this year?

Two or more “no” answers means downgrade to the no-fee sibling. A product change usually keeps your credit history and adds no new inquiry.

Never carry a balance for rewards

A single month of interest at a typical card APR erases an entire year of points. Rewards are a rebate on spending you would do anyway, not a reason to spend.

Frequently asked questions

Are points better than cash back?

Only above about 1.5 cents per point in realistic redemptions with planned transfers. Below that, cash back wins on simplicity.

Airline card or flexible card?

One airline with regular checked bags favours a co-brand. Booking by price favours a flexible or no-fee card.

What foreign fee is acceptable?

Zero, for any card you use abroad.

Do lounges justify a premium fee alone?

Rarely below about eight visits a year at realistic per-visit value. Count last year's visits first.

Should beginners start with premium cards?

No. Start with a no-fee, no-foreign-fee card and build habits. Upgrade once the maths is proven on your own travel.
