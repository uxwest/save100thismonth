Best Credit Card for Travel: Pick by Your Trips, Not the Points

Smart Shopping · Guide · For US readers · Card terms change; verify with the issuer before applying

Quick answer: The best travel card depends on how you travel. Frequent flyer loyal to one airline? An airline co-brand may win. Flexible travelers? A transferable-points card usually wins. No-fee traveler? Skip premium cards — their value comes from benefits you must actually use.

The math that decides it

A premium card only pays off if you use its credits. Add up, per year:

  • Real value used = (travel credits you’d use anyway) + (lounge visits × value to you) + (points earned × your realistic redemption value) + (foreign transaction fee savings on your actual spend).
  • Compare against the annual fee. If you can’t list the credits you’ll use, the fee is pure cost.
Example (illustrative only): If you fly twice a year domestically and never use lounges, a no-annual-fee card with no foreign transaction fees likely beats a $395 premium card. If you fly monthly and use the travel credit fully, the premium math can flip.

Checklist before applying

  1. Verify the current annual fee, welcome offer and spending requirement on the issuer’s own page.
  2. Check foreign transaction fees if you travel abroad.
  3. Check which transfer partners matter for airlines you actually fly.
  4. Read the fine print on travel credits — many are portal-only or hotel-specific.
  5. Never carry a balance to earn points: interest rates dwarf any rewards.

Common mistakes

  • Valuing points at marketing rates instead of your realistic redemption.
  • Keeping a card a second year after the credits stopped being useful.
  • Opening cards purely for bonuses without a plan to meet the minimum spend responsibly.

FAQ

Are points worth more than cash back?

Only if you redeem them well. If you’d cash out at low value, a flat cash-back card is often simpler and better.

Should I get an airline card or a flexible card?

Flexible cards win if you switch airlines; co-brands win if you always fly one airline and value its perks (free checked bag, boarding).
