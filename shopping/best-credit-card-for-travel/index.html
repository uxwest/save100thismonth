Best travel credit card: the verdict for your travel style

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialUpdated Oct 10, 202611 min read

There is no single best travel card, but there is one correct answer for you, and it depends entirely on how you travel.

This guide gives that verdict up front, then shows how to verify the current offer and avoid overpaying.

The fee-versus-value equation

Add only the value you would actually use. Then subtract the annual fee.

net value = usable credits + lounge value + (points x your redemption) + foreign fee savings - annual fee rule: if it is negative two years in a row, downgrade

A premium card justifies its fee through three things: travel credits, lounge access and status perks. Use two of the three and the maths works. Use none and you paid for a metal ornament.

The vague list problem. If you cannot name the credits you will use this year, the fee is pure cost. Do not apply on hope.

Foreign transaction fees: non-negotiable

A 3% foreign fee on $5,000 of overseas spending costs $150 a year — more than many annual fees. Any card you carry across a border must charge zero percent.

  • Always choose to pay in the local currency (avoid bundle conversion)
  • Check ATM policies abroad, which differ from purchase rules
  • Confirm "no foreign fee" covers cash-like transactions too

Lounges: count visits, not prestige

Two domestic trips a year with short layovers might mean two visits. At $30 of value each, that is $60 against a $395 fee.

Rarely under about eight visits a year is a lounge worth paying for. Audit last year's itineraries before you buy prestige.

How to verify the current offer (2 minutes)

  1. Identify your family from the table above.
  2. Open the issuer's own page for the current fee and welcome offer.
  3. Cross-check on NerdWallet or The Points Guy.
  4. Confirm three deal-breakers: 0% FX fee, credits you will use, a bonus you can earn from normal spending.
Never manufactured spend. The bonus must come from normal purchases. That is how points become debt.

FAQ

Are points better than cash back?

Only above about 1.5 cents per point with planned transfers. Below that, cash back wins on simplicity.

Airline card or flexible card?

One airline with regular checked bags favors a co-brand. Booking by price favors a flexible or no-fee card.

What is a good travel credit score?

Most premium cards favor good-to-excellent credit. Check your free score before applying and space applications.

What foreign fee is acceptable?

Zero, for any card you use abroad. 3% on $5,000 of overseas spend costs $150 a year.
