Best travel credit card: the verdict for your exact travel style

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialUpdated October 10, 202611 min read

There is no single "best travel credit card," but there is a single correct answer for you — and it depends entirely on how you travel. This guide gives you the verdict up front, organized by traveler profile, then shows how to verify the offer.

The fastest answer, right now
  • You fly one airline 5+ times a year and always check a bag: airline co-brand — the free bag and boarding alone can repay the fee.
  • You book whatever is cheapest, 3+ times a year: transferable-points card you actually use.
  • You travel 1–2 times a year: no-annual-fee card with no foreign transaction fee.
  • You travel abroad: non-negotiable — zero percent foreign transaction fee on every option above.

The fee-versus-value equation

Add only the value you would buy anyway. Then subtract the annual fee.

net value = usable credits + lounge value + (points x your redemption) + foreign fee savings - annual fee rule: if it is negative two years in a row, downgrade

Premium cards justify their fee through three things: travel credits, lounge access and status perks. Use two of the three and the maths works. Use none and you have bought an expensive ornament.

Foreign transaction fees: non-negotiable

A 3% foreign transaction fee on $5,000 of annual overseas spending costs $150 — more than many annual fees. Any card you carry across a border must charge zero percent.

Also check

  • Dynamic currency conversion — always choose to pay in the local currency
  • ATM policies abroad, which often differ from purchase rules
  • Whether "no foreign fee" covers purchases only or cash-like transactions too

Lounges: count visits, not prestige

Unlimited access sounds luxurious until you count. Two domestic trips a year with short layovers might mean two visits. At a generous $30 of perceived value each, that is $60 against a $395 fee. Frequent long-haul travellers extract hundreds; everyone else subsidises them.

How to verify the current offer (2 minutes)

  1. Identify the family for your profile from the table above.
  2. Search the issuer's own page for its current annual fee and welcome offer.
  3. Cross-check on a comparison site like NerdWallet or The Points Guy for similar offers and fine-print reviews.
  4. Confirm the three deal-breakers: 0% FX fee, credits you will actually use, and a welcome offer whose minimum spend you can meet from normal budgets.
Never manufactured spendThe minimum spend for a bonus must come from normal purchases. That is how points turn into debt.

Never carry a balance for rewards

A single month of interest at a typical card APR erases an entire year of points. Rewards are a rebate on spending you would do anyway, never a reason to spend.

Frequently asked questions

Are points better than cash back?

Only above about 1.5 cents per point in realistic redemptions with planned transfers. Below that, cash back wins on simplicity.

Airline card or flexible card?

One airline with regular checked bags favours a co-brand. Booking by price favours a flexible or no-fee card.

What is a good travel credit score to apply with?

Most premium travel cards favour good-to-excellent credit. Check your free score before applying, and space applications rather than clustering them.

What foreign fee is acceptable?

Zero, for any card you use abroad. 3% on $5,000 of overseas spend costs $150 a year.

Do lounges justify a premium fee alone?

Rarely below about eight visits a year at realistic per-visit value. Count last year's visits first.

Should beginners start with premium cards?

No. Start with a no-fee, no-foreign-fee card and build habits. Upgrade once the maths is proven on your own travel.
