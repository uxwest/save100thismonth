Best meal delivery: kits vs prepared vs restaurant apps

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialUpdated October 10, 202610 min read

Food is the most elastic line in most household budgets. Delivery is where it stretches fastest.

A restaurant delivery order is not just the menu price. It is a marked-up menu price, plus service fee, plus delivery fee, plus a tip on everything above.

This guide does the arithmetic honestly and helps you match a model to how your actual week works.

The pattern is consistent across markets: batch cooking is cheapest, kits sit in the middle, prepared meals cost more, restaurant delivery is most expensive once every fee and tip is counted.

The real question is never “which is cheapest.” It is “which do I actually follow through on.” A cheap plan you abandon is the most expensive plan of all.

Even with generous ranges, the direction is unmistakable. Cutting restaurant delivery from four nights a week to once a month is one of the largest single levers available in a normal household budget.

It is not about never ordering in. It is noticing that the expensive default has quietly become automatic.

Fees and markups to look for specifically

  • Menu markup. Compare the app price to the restaurant's own menu. 20 to 30% is common, and you pay it before any fee appears.
  • Service fee. Often a percentage.
  • Delivery fee. Varies by distance and demand, and rises exactly when you want to order.
  • Small-order fee. Applied below a threshold the app chooses.
  • Tip. Expected on top of everything, calculated on the inflated subtotal.
  • Delivery subscriptions. Only pay off above a certain order frequency.
The quiet trapA delivery subscription can make ordering feel free, which increases how often you order. If you order twice as much because delivery was “already paid for,” the subscription cost you money.

Meal kits: worth it, and for whom

Meal kits solve one specific problem: deciding what to cook and shopping for it. If those two tasks are why you do not cook, the per-serving premium buys something real.

What it does not do is beat groceries on price, and promotional pricing is temporary. Before committing, find the skip-week deadline in your account, read what happens if you miss it and confirm the post-promotion price.

The single most useful piece of information for a kit subscriber is the date by which they must act each week.

Prepared meals: paying for time

Prepared meals also save the cooking, which is why they cost more per serving.

They make sense when your time is genuinely scarce, or when specific dietary requirements would otherwise mean ordering out.

Watch for portions that leave you hungry, subscription minimums that lock you into more meals than you eat, and the gap between “fresh” and “frozen” ranges, which is often just a price difference rather than a quality one.

A hybrid most households can sustain

Rather than picking one model forever, set a default and carve out exceptions.

  1. Batch cook on a normal week. Default mode.
  2. Keep one kit for chaotic weeks. The ones you know are coming.
  3. Allow delivery once a month, deliberately. A treat, not a fallback.

This structure keeps the savings without demanding the discipline that makes strict plans collapse.

Frequently asked questions

Are meal kits cheaper than groceries?

Rarely per serving. Their value is time saved and waste avoided. Whether that trade is worth it depends on how you behave, not the sticker price.

How do I cancel a meal kit cleanly?

Find the weekly cutoff in your account settings and pause or cancel before it. That cutoff is the most important detail for any kit subscriber, and it is usually buried.

Is delivery-app food more expensive than ordering directly?

Usually yes. Compare the app price with the restaurant's menu, then add service fee, delivery fee and tip. Ordering directly and picking up often costs much less.

Is a delivery subscription worth it?

Only if you order often enough that waived fees exceed the subscription cost, and only if it does not cause you to order more often.

What is the biggest saving available here?

Reducing how often you order delivery. It is consistently the most expensive per meal once all fees and tips are counted.
