Best antiviral for Mac: do you actually need one?

By Save100ThisMonth EditorialUpdated Oct 10, 202610 min read

Ask a Mac owner and you get two opposite answers, both right. The honest answer depends on how you use the machine.

macOS ships with genuinely effective protection. A careful user may never need to pay. A shared family machine is a different case.

Verdict by how you use your Mac

Your situation vs. what you need
You…What you need
Install only from the App Store or trusted makersBuilt-in macOS protection is enough
Share the Mac with familyThird-party adds a useful layer
Install from random sites, click everythingPaid protection plus better habits

What macOS already does for free

Gatekeeper

Blocks unsigned or tampered apps before they run. That is the "cannot verify developer" message.

XProtect

Apple's built-in malware signature system, updated independently of full updates.

Sandboxing + system integrity protection

Limit what a compromised app can reach, and block changes to critical system files.

When paid software earns its price

  • You install software from sources you do not verify
  • The Mac is shared with unpredictable clickers
  • You want browser-level phishing and web filtering
  • You run Windows in a virtual machine
Buy extra protection when it addresses your real behaviour, not when an ad says your Mac is unprotected.

How to compare products

Four factors, four traps
CriterionThe trap
Independent Mac-specific test resultsVendors citing Windows-only results
Renewal price, years 2 and 3Cheap intro, expensive renewal
What you already ownPaying twice for a VPN you have

Free habits that beat most paid tools

  • Keep macOS updated so XProtect stays current
  • Use a password manager, never reuse credentials
  • Turn on two-factor on email, banking and recovery accounts
  • Treat surprise pop-ups and "call this number" warnings as scams
Common trap. Fake "your Mac is infected, call this number" pop-ups. Real software does not ask you to phone a support line from a web page.

General information for US readers.

FAQ

Can Macs get viruses?

Yes, but the profile differs from Windows. For most users the biggest risk is phishing.

Is built-in protection enough?

For careful users installing from trusted sources, usually yes.

Do I need a VPN too?

Not automatically. Many people already pay for one through work or a bundle.
